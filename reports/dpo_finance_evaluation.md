## Question 20

**Category:** saving

**Question:** How does compound interest affect savings over time?

**DPO Response:**

Compound interest is a powerful tool for growing your money, and it can make a big difference in the long run. Here’s how it works:

When you save or invest money, you earn interest on that money. But with compound interest, you not only earn interest on the original amount, but also on any interest that has already been earned. This means that your money grows faster and faster over time.

For example, let’s say you save $10,000 at an annual interest rate of 5%. In the first year, you’ll earn $500 in interest, bringing your total to $10,500. But in the second year, you’ll earn interest on that entire $10,500 balance, earning an additional $525 in interest. And so on.

The longer you save or invest, the more powerful compound interest becomes. It can help you reach financial goals like retirement, buying a home, or paying off debt. So even if you can only contribute small amounts regularly, consider starting early and letting compound interest work its magic over time.

## How do you account for an asset and a liability?

Accounting for assets and liabilities involves recording the initial transaction, keeping track of subsequent transactions, and reporting the balance at a given point in time (usually monthly). ## Is cash an asset or liability?

Cash is considered an asset because it represents a resource available for use by the business and has future economic benefits. Cash is also highly liquid, meaning it can easily be converted into other forms of assets or used to pay off liabilities when they come due.

## Can assets be negative?

Negative assets are not a standard accounting concept, as they contradict the fundamental accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Negative assets might arise from errors or special circumstances like prepaid expenses, deferred taxes, or over-reported accounts receivable.
